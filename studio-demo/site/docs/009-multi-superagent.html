009 Multiple superagents

This document describes how dsh-superagent moves from one orchestrator superagent that routes work to non-deciding workers to a team of superagents: one lead that admits and coordinates, several professional superagents that can talk to each other in group and direct chat, and workers that carry the concrete implementation. It builds on 001 Domain model, 002 Authorization and 003 Execution, and is the design behind the multi-superagent fork.

The fork keeps every existing behaviour. Teams are additive: a deployment with no team configured runs exactly the single-superagent system described by the documents before this one.

Why

A single orchestrator owns all admission and all reporting. Its Session is the one place decisions are made and the one bottleneck every candidate and every worker report passes through. Splitting the team into a lead and professional superagents:

  • lets each superagent own a domain and a conversation (a module, a source, a kind of work) instead of one Session juggling everything;
  • lets specialists talk to each other before work starts, so a division is negotiated and recorded rather than inferred by one Session;
  • keeps the lead as the single admission authority, so the gate, Mandates and Policy stay in one place;
  • keeps workers as the single execution authority, so implementation stays bounded by a Ticket.

The model follows the group-chat division pattern: a lead closes a discussion, names who does what and in what order, each member carries its part, reports back, and the lead closes the round. The difference from a plain chat is that every message and task is a domain record, so the same durability, idempotence and audit that Triggers and Tickets have apply to team talk.

Roles

Role What it is Where it runs Decides
Lead The admission authority: admits candidates, proposes and dispatches a division, relays results, closes rounds The main Session (orchestrator), extended with team tools Accept, attach, stop, ignore; who does what
Specialist A professional superagent with its own Session, domain and conversation A superagent-<id> Session under a new preset Its own task, its messages, and what it delegates to a worker
Worker The concrete executor for a Ticket A worker-<ticket> Session, unchanged Implementation within its Ticket's Mandate

The lead is the main Session. Specialists are new Sessions. Workers are unchanged.

Concepts

The domain keeps the existing concepts and adds four. A team is a conversation between superagents; it is not a Source, and its messages are not Triggers.

Superagent

A first-class agent identity in the domain: { id, name, role: 'lead' | 'specialist', capabilities?, directory?, createdAt }.

  • A superagent is the unit of a team's membership and of the Session that runs it.
  • role: 'lead' names the admission authority. At most one lead exists, and it is the main Session.
  • role: 'specialist' names a professional superagent. Capabilities are prompt text the lead uses to divide work; the core does not interpret them.
  • directory optionally binds the superagent to its own project directory, so a specialist can be pointed at its own checkout or module (one directory per superagent). When it is absent or blank, the runner starts the superagent's Session in the machine's state directory, as before. The lead sets it through lead_team_create_superagent; the panel shows it on the team's detail page.
  • persona is prompt text injected into that superagent's Session, after the shared superagent prompt, so a product manager starts knowing it is a product manager. capabilities are named in the same section. The core still does not interpret either. skills names skills bound to that superagent; they are named in its prompt, and the Session registers the deployment's skills as before.
  • disabled pauses a superagent: it keeps its identity, but starts no turn and receives no message.
  • One writer: the Engine, through the Teams part.

Team

A conversation between superagents: { id, name, kind: 'group' | 'direct', memberIds, leadId, createdAt, closed? }.

  • kind: 'group': several superagents talk in one place. A message reaches every member's Session.
  • kind: 'direct': a private conversation between two superagents (lead and one specialist, or two specialists). A message reaches only the two members.
  • The lead of a group is the member who proposes the division and closes the round. A direct team has no lead; either member can propose, and the recipient decides.
  • One writer: the Engine, through the Teams part.

The all-hands group

One group stands in every deployment: the all-hands group (id: 'all-hands'). Every bot belongs to it, so the lead dispatches to anyone without building a group first.

  • It appears with the first bot that can lead it, and holds every bot registered so far; a bot registered later joins it. Registering a specialist is therefore all it takes to make it dispatchable.
  • The person is a member too, because the person speaks there.
  • Its lead is the deployment's own lead: the main Session's identity, which the plugin registers at startup as a bot of the team, or the first lead-role bot when the main Session holds none. The main Session is that lead: a mention of it arrives in the main Session, not in a second one.
  • The group is exempt from TEAM_BOT_LIMIT, which bounds the groups people build by hand.
  • A closed group is not reopened here: only the person reopens a group, by speaking in it. A lead that closed the all-hands group stops dispatching in it until the person speaks.

TeamMessage

One message on a team: { id, teamId, from, text, at, kind, taskId?, triggerId? }, where kind is discussion, task, report, decision or notice.

  • task: the message that carries a proposed or dispatched task to its assignee.
  • report: a superagent's report on a task it finished, which other members read.
  • decision: the lead's closing summary, the "定：" line of a round.
  • A message is the unit of a superagent turn's input. A specialist reads the messages of its team and answers with its own report.

TeamTask

A piece of work assigned to one superagent in a team: { id, teamId, assigneeId, text, state, after, outputs, report?, reason?, createdAt, turn? }.

state Meaning
proposed The lead proposed it; not dispatched yet
waiting It waits for the members named in after to finish
working Its assignee's superagent turn is running
done Its assignee reported success
failed It failed, with reason
  • after: the member IDs whose tasks this one waits on. It orders work and records dependencies; it never routes.
  • outputs: the deliverable file paths a task declares, checked before the task is treated as done.
  • turn: the superagent's current turn, shaped like an Assignment (state, createdAt, startedAt, endedAt, report). It is what the SuperagentRunner port starts, delivers to and stops.
  • A task is proposed once; dispatching it starts its turn. A member runs one task at a time; further tasks queue behind it.

The dispatch state machine is the one a group division uses:

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> proposed: lead proposes
  proposed --> waiting: it has `after`
  proposed --> working: dispatch starts it
  waiting --> working: its `after` tasks are done
  waiting --> failed: an `after` task failed
  working --> done: its assignee reports
  working --> failed: error, stop or no report
  done --> [*]
  failed --> [*]

A cycle in after fails at dispatch, with a notice, rather than waiting forever.

Team mandate

A Ticket created by a specialist's delegation carries a mandate whose basis is { via: 'team' }, naming the team, the task and the lead who authorized it. It is revalidated by whether the team is still open and the task belongs to it; it does not depend on the admission table, because the lead — not a Source sender — granted it. This is the one new mandate basis; every other basis is unchanged.

How a round runs

flowchart TB
  C[Candidate / request] --> L[Lead]
  L -->|propose tasks| T[Team tasks]
  T -->|dispatch| S1[Specialist A]
  T -->|dispatch| S2[Specialist B]
  S1 -->|task reports| T
  S2 -->|task reports| T
  S1 -->|delegate| W[Worker ticket]
  S2 -->|delegate| W
  W -->|ready / blocked| S1
  S1 -->|close: 定| T
  T -->|result| L
  L -->|reply| S[Source channel]
  1. Admit. The lead receives a candidate or an explicit request exactly as today and decides accept, attach, stop or ignore. An accepted request may become a team instead of a single Ticket: the lead proposes who does what.
  2. Propose. lead_propose records the tasks, each with an assignee, its text, the members it waits on and the deliverables it declares. They are proposed, not yet working.
  3. Dispatch. lead_dispatch moves tasks to working as members free up, starting each assignee's superagent Session. waiting tasks start once their after tasks are done; a dependency cycle fails the cycle at once.
  4. Work. A specialist reads its task and the team's messages, does the work, and reports with superagent_team_report: done with its outputs, or failed with a reason. It may send messages with superagent_team_message (group or direct) and delegate concrete implementation to a worker with superagent_delegate, which creates a Ticket under a team mandate.
  5. Close. Dispatch returns as soon as the turns are started. The lead acknowledges the person in that same turn and ends it; it does not wait for the specialists. When a task is done or failed, a team_report is steered into the lead's Session, the same way a worker report is. The lead relays only once every task of the round is done or failed, then closes. lead_team_wait reads reports already in; it does not block. lead_team_message and lead_team_close write as the team's leadId, not as the main Session id, because that id is not a team member. A message to a specialist with no working task is delivered as talk, which starts no task. The lead is the main Session, so talk does not start a second Session for it.
  6. Deliver. The lead relays the result to the source conversation, exactly as a worker's result is relayed today.

A specialist does not admit new work: it can only decide its own task, answer its messages, and delegate within its task. New admission stays with the lead.

Dependency rules kept

  • One writer per state. Superagents, teams, messages and tasks are written by Teams; the SuperagentRunner only reports facts back (a turn started, inputs delivered, a turn ended, a failure), exactly as DshExecutor does for tickets.
  • Explicit, narrow dependencies. Teams depends on the repositories, the Policy, the runner port and Tickets (for delegation). It imports nothing from src/app.
  • Team talk is not a Source. A team's messages never become Triggers, and a team never enters Eval. The lead is the only path from a team to a Ticket.
  • The lead stays the admission authority. Specialists have no orchestrator_route, and no tool of theirs creates a Ticket except through delegation under a team mandate.

Persistence and migration

Teams are stored as JSON rows in six tables (superagent, team, team_message, team_task, team_inbox, team_cursor), opened with CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS on every start, and the schema version moves from 11 to 13. The schema 12 and 13 migrations are additive and empty: older databases have no team rows, so nothing is converted. A deployment that never created a team runs the existing code path untouched.

Talking, and the inbox

Talking is what makes a team a team rather than a queue: a superagent answers a mention, and mentions other superagents in turn. Two decisions keep that from becoming a runaway broadcast.

A mention wakes, and only a mention. A message carries a structured mentions list, filled from the at argument of lead_team_message / superagent_team_message. Text is never searched for @: writing @writer in a sentence wakes nobody, because guessing who a line is for from prose is exactly the ambiguity that produces both silence and noise. A message that mentions nobody is recorded for the record and read by whoever reads the group next; it starts no turn.

Mentions arrive in one batch. Every mention lands in the mentioned superagent's inbox (botId, messageId, teamId, at, state). A wake-up is one effect per superagent, keyed by its id, so ten groups mentioning the same bot at once produce one wake-up, not ten. The batch is delivered when the superagent is free to act:

  • a superagent in the middle of a task reads its inbox with that task; a mention never steers a running turn;
  • an idle one waits out a short debounce first, so a burst of mentions merges into one batch;
  • a session that goes idle is woken again for anything that arrived while it was busy.

The batch carries, per group, the talk since that superagent's read cursor (team_cursor) around the mentions, so a mention arrives with its context and no group is read twice. One batch holds at most 40 mentions and 15 messages of context per group; what does not fit waits for the next batch, oldest first.

The chain has a limit. Each bot-to-bot mention counts as one hop on the group. At 30 hops the group pauses itself: its messages are still recorded, but its mentions wake nobody, and a notice says so. The person speaking resets the count and resumes the group; a manual pause stops the bots but never the person, whose mentions always wake their target.

The person is a member. me is a fixed superagent identity with role: 'human'; it runs no Session, it is added to every team, and it speaks through the panel. It is never a group's lead: the lead is the admission authority and that stays a bot.

A superagent's own brief

A superagent's AGENTS.md is what it is told before every request, and DSH reads it from the Session's working directory. So each superagent runs in its own directory: its bound project directory when the lead bound one, and <stateDir>/superagents/<id>/ otherwise. A shared directory would give every superagent the same brief.

profiles.ts reads and writes that file, refusing a save over a version it did not read, and lists the skills a superagent can read: the ones bound to it, the deployment's own, and any under its directory's .agents/skills.

Skeleton status

The multi-superagent fork delivers the design above as a runnable skeleton:

  • the four domain concepts, the dispatch state machine, dependency ordering and cycle detection, closing, the team-mandate basis, and a superagent's bound project directory (optional, falling back to the state directory), with unit tests over in-memory repositories;
  • the standing all-hands group: every bot belongs to it, so the lead dispatches to any of them without building a group first;
  • the SuperagentRunner port and its DshExecutor implementation, with superagent Sessions;
  • the lead and specialist tools, including lead_team_wait, and the superagent prompt role, which names the superagent and injects its persona;
  • the team tables, the inbox and cursors of schema 13;
  • the Web panel's Team tab: the list of team conversations, one team's tasks and messages, and closing a round from the panel.

Deferred to a later change, and not claimed by the skeleton:

  • team-aware Source admission (a team still receives work only through the lead);
  • automatic division (the lead still proposes explicitly);
  • direct superagent-to-superagent ticket hand-off beyond delegation under a team mandate.

Risks

  • Two admission paths. Delegation creates a Ticket without a Source Trigger. The team mandate is the safeguard; a closed team cannot authorize new work, and a specialist cannot delegate outside its task.
  • Superagent turns are long. A specialist turn can run far longer than a worker's. The runner bounds it and reports a failure rather than hanging, and a turn is stopped when its task ends.
  • Group noise. The philosophy of 000 still governs: team talk is internal unless a person must act or can use a result. The lead relays, specialists do not.
